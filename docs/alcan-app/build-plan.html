The Alcan App

One app that is the front door to work life at Alcan. Staff open it to catch up on what's happening, do their Pro Moves work, ask questions and get real answers, submit content, and jump to every other tool they use. Pro Moves becomes a feature of the Alcan App, not the container.

Prepared Sept 24, 2026 Owner John Status Reviewed & largely approved by Tim and Dr. Alex Starting point Pro Moves as it runs today

Where we start

This is not a rebuild. The platform staff use every week keeps running untouched while each phase adds around it. Much of the Alcan App already exists: the coaching loop is live, the Ask Alcan answer engine is built and being tested, and push notifications are already planned. The genuinely new pieces are the staff feed, the social content drop-box, and the connections to the other tools.

Every phase below ends the same way: something working that we demonstrate to Tim and Alex on a real phone, not a document.

Ground rules for the whole build

Never break TuesdayNew surfaces are built alongside the current ones. Staff never lose a working feature mid-transition.
Proven over scratchEvery new capability copies a pattern that already works elsewhere, backed by a research memo. We assemble proven parts; we don't invent.
One app, both screensPhone-first web app you install from the browser, and a full desktop experience. No app store, nothing changes that.
Alcan on top, platform underneathThe platform stays multi-practice under the hood (Avenue runs on it). The Alcan App is Alcan's branded front door, never a fork.
Feed, not forum, not chatThe communication layer is a staff feed: announcements with comments and read-tracking. Person-to-person chat deliberately stays out of the app.
Retire Basecamp onceBasecamp's two jobs, documents and communication, each get a new home. We turn Basecamp off one time, when both are ready.
Use what we already pay forAlcan runs on Google Workspace. Before building or buying any supporting piece, we check whether Drive, Docs, Gemini, or Apps Script already does it well — everywhere in the plan, not just social media.
The app is the staff sign-inMost staff don't have Google accounts and won't get them. Staff sign in to the app the way they do today; anything Google-powered runs behind the scenes, never behind a Google login staff don't have.

1

The front door

One clean shell around everything that already exists.

What happens

  • A deliberate redesign of the app's navigation. Today's desktop nav grew one item per feature; this phase reorganizes everything into the layout proven employee apps converge on: Home, Community, Hub, My Growth, Profile, with a matching full desktop layout.
  • The Hub launches with tiles for Done Desk, ADP, Uptime, and Deputy: one tap out to everything else. Google Chat tiles appear only for the folks who have Google accounts (mostly leadership); everyone else's quick back-and-forth stays where it already lives, texting.
  • Design mockups first, build second.

What staff notice

The app gets a new coat of paint and a simpler layout. Everything they already use still works, in a more obvious place, plus one screen that links to every other work tool.

2

The library

Basecamp exit, part one: documents move to their permanent home.

What happens

  • The curated keeper documents from the Basecamp harvest move into the Shared Drive, organized by who's allowed to see what. Your sorting ledger is the moving checklist.
  • Ask Alcan's nightly sync repoints from Basecamp to Drive, so editing a Google Doc updates the bot's knowledge within a day.
  • Since most staff have no Google account, the app itself becomes their reading room: tapping a citation opens the document inside the app, rendered from the same synced copy the bot reads. Drive is where leadership writes and organizes; the app is where staff read. Nobody ever hits a Google login wall.
  • Expert sign-off on the corpus finishes, and Ask opens to a pilot group beyond the current test gate.

What staff notice

The pilot group gets Ask Alcan: type a question, get a cited answer, tap the citation to read the actual policy, right there in the app.

3

The feed

Basecamp exit, part two: communication moves in, and Basecamp turns off.

What happens

  • Push notifications go live (already fully planned), because a feed nobody is told about is a bulletin board in a basement.
  • The staff feed is built: posts from leadership and locations, comments and reactions underneath, and read-tracking on the must-reads ("12 of 15 have acknowledged"). Audiences by location and role.
  • Concept art already exists for this screen (the "News tab" exploration: a calm briefing rather than a social stream, must-see posts you tap "Got it" on, and a monthly "Vitals" letter from Tim). It's a design input for the checkpoint below, not a committed spec.
  • No chat, no direct messages, by design. Quick back-and-forth stays where it already happens: texting for most staff, Google Chat for the leaders who have accounts. Either way, retention and moderation never become the app's problem.
  • Pilot at one location, tune, roll out, then retire Basecamp.

What staff notice

Home becomes a real feed. Announcements arrive as notifications on their phone, and Basecamp quietly disappears.

4

The studio

Social media content flows from staff phones to the social manager's Drive folder.

What happens

  • A submit screen: pick photos or video, add a note, hit send. Files land directly in a dedicated Shared Drive folder for the social media manager.
  • Uploads run in the background with a progress bar, because chairside wifi and 2-gigabyte videos are both real. Staff never need a Google login; the files travel through our backend into Drive.
  • This reuses the exact Drive plumbing phase 2 builds, which is why it's cheap and why it comes after.
  • Worth evaluating here (we already pay for it): Gemini auto-describing and tagging each arrival, so the social manager's folder sorts itself instead of filling with IMG_4021.mov.

5

The connections

Deputy stops being just a link.

What happens

  • "My shifts" appears in the app, read straight from Deputy, and Ask Alcan learns to answer schedule questions with real roster context.
  • Research confirmed clock-in and clock-out are possible through Deputy's official interfaces, so that's a genuine later option, taken only if the link version proves annoying.
  • Done Desk, ADP, and Uptime stay links unless usage argues otherwise. Links that work beat modules that lag.

Parallel track: our own ground

Moving off Lovable onto company-owned hosting. Runs alongside the phases, invisible to staff.

What happens

  • Today the app publishes through Lovable. The existing consolidation plan moves it to infrastructure the company owns and controls: deploys happen when we merge, not when a button is pressed.
  • Rehearse on the low-stakes Summit site first, then move this app, then flip the new domain and finally make the code repository private.
  • Timing recommendation: complete the move before phase 3. The feed and push era needs us in full control of releases, and the domain flip should land with the new name.

How we'll work

Every ticket runs through the existing kit with its two human gates: you approve the spec before anything is built, and you review and merge the finished work. Between those gates the build and QA chain runs without interruptions. On top of that sit the amber checkpoints above: the moments where Tim and Alex see the thing and steer. Each phase ships to a pilot group before it ships to everyone, and each phase ends with a live demo on a phone.

Settled since v1

  • The plan itself. Tim and Alex reviewed v1 and largely approved it (Sept 24).
  • Workspace identities. Most staff do not get Google accounts, and won't. The plan above has been reworked so nothing staff-facing depends on one: sign-in stays the app's own, documents are read inside the app, and Google does its work behind the scenes.

Still open

  • The name and domain. Phase 0's headline decision, with Tim.
  • Feed build approach. Recommendation is a small build on our own stack; the honest alternative (a feed service's free tier) is in the research memo. Concept art for the briefing-style News tab exists and feeds the design checkpoint.
  • Whether the Shared Drive exists yet. Unconfirmed; phase 0 verifies or creates it.
  • Exact hosting-move timing. Recommended before phase 3; the rehearsal on Summit will tell us how confident to be.
  • Where else Google earns a place. A standing question at each phase, per the new ground rule: Gemini for social-upload tagging, Docs for authoring posts and Vitals, Apps Script on the Shared Drive.
