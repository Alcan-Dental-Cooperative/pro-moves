# Avenue training day: facilitator agenda

**Tuesday 6 October 2026.** Built from the training arc v0 (session state,
2026-09-16) and journey doc v0.3 (`docs/alcan-avenue-patient-journey.md`).
Casting slots marked **⟨TBD⟩** get filled from Bobby's answers: nurse roster
(Q4), therapist attendance (Q6), PM in post (Q1), full closure (Q5).

**The principle, stated once:** the day trains the seams, not role depth.
Everything that fails at Avenue fails *between* people, so every role-play is a
handoff, run in the real rooms, with the people who will actually do it.

**Facilitation:** John leads, Tim co-facilitates and keeps time. Bobby attends
only as the dentist inside role-plays. He stays in character during scenes and
saves all coaching for debriefs (rehearse this with him beforehand, see prep
checklist). No other doctors attend; how associates hear the changes is a
separate conversation with Bobby.

## Session run sheets

### 1. The why, and finishing the manifesto (09:00, 45 min)

**Purpose:** everyone leaves this session able to say what is changing and why
it is good for patients and for them, in their own words.

**Run:**
- 10 min: John. The one-page operating shift: today Avenue runs on whoever is
  free, and nobody owns the patient. The new standard is a named owner: one
  person who introduces themselves at first contact and is still there at the
  last one [TCO-NARR]. Second shift: hygiene becomes the front door of the
  routine journey, not an afterthought.
- 5 min: honest status check on the already-running standards (10/20
  acknowledgement, no prices on the phone, why-now/why-us/referral capture,
  the 5-attempts cadence, backlog calling). Hands up, no blame, just "where
  are we really". This sets the tone that truth is safe today.
- 20 min: finish the manifesto WITH the staff. Pairs write one line each for
  "what we promise a patient who chooses us". Collect on the wall. The
  manifesto script (build list item 12) leaves the day half-written by them,
  which is the point: handed-down values do not survive contact with a busy
  Tuesday.
- 10 min: questions, open floor.

**Facilitator note:** if the contracts conversation landed badly (Bobby Q9),
this session grows and the tone shifts toward security: nobody loses a job,
roles get narrower and clearer, not bigger. Have that version ready.

### 2. Two patients walk through the practice (09:45, 45 min)

**Purpose:** everyone sees the whole journey once, physically, before drilling
any piece of it. Seams get named as seams while standing where they happen.

**Run:** John narrates, the group literally walks the building.
- Patient one, Track A: "Mr Smith", who rings asking how much an implant is.
  Walk: phone rings (front desk), first call and discovery (TCO space),
  arrival and greeting, the consultation (consultation room), findings and
  ballpark, close, exam day and the warm handoff (surgery), signup before he
  leaves.
- Patient two, Track B: a family who just moved to the area. Walk: the
  booking call (hygiene first, pre-booked for every new patient), the hygiene
  visit, the call-in when the therapist spots something, then the dentist,
  arriving already cleaned, already educated.
- At every handoff, stop walking and say "this is a seam, this is where
  patients get lost today". The afternoon drills exactly these stops.

**Facilitator note:** keep it story, not standards. The numbers (61% of
contacted leads never reaching CareStack) can be used once, here, to show the
leak is real, then leave the data alone for the rest of the day.

### 3. Seam block A: the enquiry seam (10:45, 75 min)

**Purpose:** install the front edge of Track A: the catch, the first call, and
the seam between them.

**Part 1, the first call (40 min).** Cast: Millie as TCO, rotating patients.
- Anchor script, when someone opens with "how much is an implant":
  "Thanks so much for getting in touch, Mr Smith. Before I answer that, can
  you tell me a bit more about what's going on? Is it for yourself?"
  Never: "Our implants start from £X." Prices are not quoted on the phone.
- What the call must do: discovery (what is going on, why now, why us),
  referral source captured, CareStack record created on THIS call,
  consultation booked with the £35 refundable deposit, no minimum lead time.
- Scenarios, in order: (1) the price shopper, (2) the nervous patient,
  (3) the friend referral (the call opens differently), (4) talk-through
  only: the lead who does not answer, and what the 5-attempts-over-10-days
  cadence looks like in practice.

**Part 2, the catch and the callback (30 min).** Cast: Angelene (and ⟨Abbie?⟩
if she carries catch duty, Bobby Q3) plus Millie.
- The seam: TCO is with a patient, phone falls through to reception.
  Reception acknowledges within 10/20, takes name, number, and what it is
  about, quotes no prices, promises the TCO will call within the hour, and
  hands it off cleanly. Then Millie runs the callback rep.
- Run it at least three times: clean catch, catch while the desk is busy,
  and a caller who pushes hard for a price at the catch.

**Debrief (5 min):** what felt unnatural, what needs a crib card at the desk.

### 4. Seam block B: the hygiene call-in (12:45, 60 min)

**This gets the most reps of the day.** It is the only behaviour in the whole
model that nobody at Avenue has ever done, and the therapist carrying it is a
fresh graduate. Run it in the actual hygiene surgery.

**Cast:** ⟨therapist, confirm attending, Bobby Q6⟩ and Millie. Patients played
by nurses in rotation.

**The behaviour:** when the therapist finds something worth a conversation,
the TCO comes in during the visit, while the patient is in the chair and the
thing they have just been shown is still on the screen. Anchor script:
"Before you head off, let me introduce you to someone. This is Millie, she
looks after patients who are thinking about exactly this sort of thing."
Millie then runs a short discovery and, if there is real interest, books a
proper consultation.

**Rounds:**
1. Slow motion, coached openly, once through.
2. Patient variants: interested, hesitant, in a hurry.
3. The edge case: Millie is with another patient. What then: the therapist
   walks the patient to the desk and a TCO conversation gets booked before
   they leave. The patient never just drifts out of the building.

**Facilitator note:** whether *every* Track B patient meets the TCO or only
those where something is found is still open (journey Q14, on Bobby's list).
Train it as "when something is found", which is where Bobby leaned, and say
so out loud if asked.

### 5. Seam block C: exam-day warm handoff (13:45, 45 min)

**Cast:** Millie, ⟨nurse, TBD from roster⟩, Bobby as the dentist, in
character. Run in a real surgery.

**The sequence, per journey A9:**
1. Millie meets and greets the returning patient. Familiarity is the point.
2. Warm handoff to nurse and dentist, formal enough that the patient
   registers it as a moment. The nurse presents the patient by name with
   procedure, history and concerns (existing nurse move 265, so this is
   refresh, not new).
3. Bobby's 10-second cameo as himself: the dentist who has clearly been
   briefed, greets the patient by name, references what they told Millie.
4. Dentist and TCO confer on staging before the patient leaves.
5. The patient goes back to Millie before leaving the building. Say the hard
   rule plainly: if the plan was formulated today, the patient does not
   leave without seeing the TCO.

**Rounds:** three. Straight run, then a patient with a concern the nurse must
carry into the presentation, then a time-pressured day where the handoff is
tempting to skip (the rep is that it still happens).

### 6. Complaint first response (14:45, 30 min)

**Purpose:** everyone can hold the first sixty seconds of an unhappy patient.

**The model:** restate what they said, then "tell me more", then an agreed
next step with a name and a time on it. Speed of response matters more than
the escalation ladder. And every complaint reaches the practice manager for
awareness, always, not for blame.

**Run:** whole group in pairs, two scenarios each way: the niggle (ran late,
patient lost time off work) and the billing query (which belongs to the TCO,
because the TCO asked for the money). Then one fishbowl: Millie takes a
billing complaint start to finish.

### 7. Demo: findings and the close (15:15, 30 min)

**Deliberately a demo, not a drill.** This is TCO craft; the room needs to
understand it, not perform it.

**Run:** John or Bobby models the A6 sequence: here is what I found, what it
is called, the remedy, a case like yours, and how it connects to what you
told me you wanted. Then the ballpark range, at this appointment, so a £49
exam never becomes a £3,000 surprise. Then the A7 close: "How do you feel
about that?" and "What would you like to do next?", and the warm hold (a
booked review, a specific follow-up with a date, never "I'll be in touch").

**Debrief prompt:** where in this conversation did this morning's first-call
discovery pay off? The answer is step five, and someone in the room will find
it without being told.

### 8. What changes for me on Monday (15:45, 30 min)

Each person writes one sentence: the one thing they do differently from
Monday. Read aloud, no discussion, cards go on the wall next to the
manifesto lines. If the practice manager is in post (Bobby Q1), they close
the session with what they will be paying attention to, framed as support.
If not in post, John frames it as "your new PM will pick these up with you",
and no further, so the day does not install authority in an empty chair.

### 9. The fortnight after, on the wall (16:15, 15 min)

The Beat 3 timeline, physically on a wall: VoiceStack routing to the TCO
line, Millie fully in role, TCO consultations in the new room, hygiene-first
as the default for new registrations, the PM running the new scorecard.
Dates on each. Bobby says the closing word as the owner of the change.
